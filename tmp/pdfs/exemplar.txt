Completed  Implementation/  Completed  Work  
The  four  emotional  environments  have  been  almost  fully  implemented,  with  each  environment  being  at  least  7  minutes  long  with  numerous  interest  points,  emotional  visual  designs,  emotional  auditor  stimulus,  participant  reactive  responses,  and  immersive  detail.    The  high  arousal,  high  valence  environment,  inducing  excitement,  is  based  on  an  otherworldly  village  and  can  be  seen  in  figure  4.  The  environment  consists  of  an  immersive  space  view,  suggested  to  induce  awe  [4],  an  active  village  scene,  floating  balloon  lanterns,  spinning  magic  lights,  massive  ships.  The  environment  also  uses  exciting  uplifting  audio,  village  sounds,  music  and  ambient  sounds  to  promote  an  exciting  emotion.   
 Figure  4.  Exciting  VR  Environment  in  experience  snapshots  

===== PAGE 6 =====
The  high  arousal,  low  valence  environment,  inducing  fear,  is  based  on  an  abandoned  psychiatric  hospital  and  can  be  seen  in  figure  5.  The  environment  consists  of  various  hallways  and  rooms  with  various  horror  props,  flickering  lighting  and  horror  SFX  audio,  including  heavy  breathing,  heartbeats,  footsteps,  creaking  wood  etc.  which  is  known  to  induce  fear  [5].  This  environment  requires  careful  consideration  as  we  want  to  avoid  causing  extreme  fear  or  discomfort  in  the  participants,  as  such  we  have  ensured  no  jumpscares  are  used  in  the  environment.    
 Figure  5.  Fear  VR  Environment  in  experience  snapshots  The  low  arousal,  high  valence  environment,  inducing  relaxation  and  calmness,  is  based  on  a  sunny  natural  location  and  can  be  seen  in  figure  6.  The  environment  consists  of  a  bright  open  natural  green  terrain,  with  various  wildlife  visible,  and  soft  glittery  particle  effects.  Waterfalls  and  mountains  are  shown  in  the  background,  which  are  known  to  induce  joy  [4],  and  nature  audio  is  used  throughout  the  experience.  
 Figure  6.  Relaxation  VR  Environment  in  experience  snapshots  The  final  environment,  low  arousal  and  low  valence,  inducing  boredom  and  depression,  is  based  on  an  abandoned  radioactive  waste  landscape,  and  can  be  seen  in  figure  7.  The  environment  consists  of  a  dreary  raining  and  mostly  grey  coloured  industrial  location,  with  various  fires,  toxic  spills,  abandoned  vehicles  and  garbage  piles.  The  audio  is  minimal  and  mainly  consists  of  ambient  noise  of  wind,  fire,  electrical  buzzing,  and  creaking  metal.

Planned  Implementation/  Future  Work  
The  environments  require  final  polishing  work  to  fix  some  of  the  implementation  bugs  and  to  add  the  final  points  of  interest  as  needed.  The  participant  questionnaire  interactions  and  the  scripts  used  to  save  participant  interaction  data  also  need  to  be  completed.  Once  this  is  completed,  the  pilot  study  can  be  run  to  test  the  environments  and  to  gain  feedback  on  the  emotion  induction  effectivity  of  each  environment.  Additionally,  we  had  planned  to  build  a  basic  VR  room  for  the  2  minute  recovery  period  instead  of  using  a  black  screen,  this  also  needs  to  be  implemented.  
Recall  System  
The  AM  recall  phase,  shown  in  figure  10,  consists  of  teleporting  participants  to  specific  locations  in  the  existing  environments  described  above,  2  days  after  the  initial  encoding  phase.  The  participant  will  be  transported  to  50  locations,  some  will  be  familiar  locations  i.e.  the  locations  at  which  a  participant  took  a  photo,  while  the  rest  will  be  unfamiliar  locations  i.e.  locations  in  the  environment  that  the  participant  never  saw.  The  participant  is  then  requested  to  indicate  if  the  location  is  familiar  or  unfamiliar  and  complete  a  self  evaluation.  This  teleporting  system  and  the  participant  interactions  is  still  to  be  implemented  and  tested.  The  implementation  will  require  using  the  photograph  interaction  data  from  the  encoding  stage  and  dummy  data  to  create  the  teleporting  system,  and  using  VR  controller  interactions  to  implement  the  participant  interactions.  Once  this  implementation  is  complete  and  the  other  project  portions  are  finalised  the  experiment  can  take  place.
