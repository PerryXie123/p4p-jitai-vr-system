[Heading 1] Introduction
[Heading 2] Background
[Normal] Exams are central to evaluating a student's academic performance, often placing them under significant pressure. This stress, combined with cognitive overload, reduces students' ability to maintain sustained attention. Internal distractions, such as mind-wandering, along with external ones like environmental noise or invigilators walking by, can significantly impair focus during exams [1][2].
[Normal] Mind-wandering is particularly problematic as it occurs when a student’s attention shifts from the task at hand to unrelated thoughts, often without their awareness [6]. This lack of self-awareness prevents students from effectively reorienting and self-correcting their attention, which ultimately harms performance. In the context of exams, this issue is compounded by stress and anxiety, which heighten cognitive load and make it even harder for students to concentrate [5][7].
[Normal] Recent advancements in immersive technologies, such as Virtual Reality (VR), provide novel ways to simulate real-world distractions and train students to maintain attention under stress. Physiological monitoring systems, such as eye-gaze tracking and heart rate variability (HRV), offer real-time insight into students' attentional states, enabling adaptive interventions that guide students back to the task when attention lapses are detected [8][9].
[Normal] Moreover, integrating Just-In-Time Adaptive Interventions (JITAI) into these systems enables personalized feedback that can help students stay focused when it matters most. JITAI frameworks use real-time data to ensure that interventions are timely and contextually relevant, improving students' self-regulation and attention control during high-pressure situations like exams [7].
[Heading 2] Research Questions
[Normal] This study explores the effectiveness of a VR-based training system incorporating JITAI to improve students' ability to focus under exam-like conditions. The research will address the following questions:
[Normal] - How effective is a VR-based training system, using eye-gaze tracking and HRV, in improving students' concentration and attention regulation under simulated exam conditions?
[Normal] - Do real-time adaptive interventions based on physiological monitoring help students re-engage with tasks after attentional lapses, and how do different types of distractions (auditory and visual) affect their performance?
[Heading 2] Aim
[Normal] The aim of this project is to design and evaluate a VR-based biofeedback system to help students regulate their attention and maintain focus under exam-like conditions. The system will integrate real-time physiological monitoring (eye-gaze tracking and HRV) to detect lapses in attention, providing adaptive feedback to guide students back to the task when their attention falters [9][10][11].
[Heading 2] Objectives
[Normal] To achieve this aim, the project will pursue the following objectives:
[Normal] Develop an Immersive VR Environment: Build a VR-based training system that simulates exam-like conditions. Initially, users will train in a calming virtual environment (e.g., a forest), with distractions (auditory and visual) gradually added to mimic real exam conditions.
[Normal] Integrate Physiological Monitoring: Implement eye-gaze tracking and HRV sensors to continuously monitor attentional focus. These measurements will guide adaptive feedback when attention lapses are detected [9][10].
[Normal] Provide Adaptive Interventions: The system will offer real-time feedback based on physiological signals (e.g., eye-gaze, HRV). These interventions will include visual cues, vignetting, soft auditory feedback, and haptic signals to help students refocus on the task [9][11].
[Normal] Evaluate System Effectiveness: The system’s effectiveness will be evaluated by comparing pre-test and post-test performance. Quantitative measures (HRV, eye-gaze) will assess attentional lapses, while qualitative evaluations will capture user experiences of focus, intervention acceptability, and anxiety [5][9].

[Heading 2] Project Scope
[Normal] This project focuses on the development and evaluation of a VR-based concentration training system within the context of exam performance. The scope includes the implementation of a single immersive training module supported by physiological monitoring through eye tracking and heart rate variability, as well as the use of adaptive interventions to guide attentional regulation in real time.
[Normal] The system is designed as a proof-of-concept prototype that demonstrates the integration of immersive environments, biofeedback, and adaptive intervention. It does not aim to develop a complete product or address long-term behavioural changes beyond the duration of the study. Additionally, the project is limited to the selected physiological signals and does not incorporate a wider range of sensing modalities.
[Normal] Testing of the system will be conducted internally within the project team and is intended solely for preliminary evaluation of system functionality and performance. As such, the study does not involve external participants, and formal ethical approval is not required.
[Normal] Furthermore, the project is constrained to the duration of this course, and no further research, large-scale evaluation, or continued development is anticipated beyond its completion.
[Heading 1] LITERATURE REVIEW
[Heading 2] Concentration, Self-Regulation, and Mind Wandering
[Normal] Sustained concentration is a critical skill for academic success, particularly in high-pressure situations like exams. However, attention is highly susceptible to distractions, particularly mind-wandering, which significantly reduces attentional control and impairs task performance. Mind-wandering refers to the shift in attention from the task at hand to unrelated thoughts, and it often occurs without the individual’s awareness [1]. This lack of self-awareness further complicates the ability to self-correct and refocus attention, resulting in performance decrements [6].
[Normal] The increase in cognitive load during exams and the presence of stress exacerbate mind-wandering, making it even harder for students to maintain focus [5][6]. As cognitive load increases, self-regulation becomes more difficult, and attention control is compromised. Research has demonstrated that students experiencing high levels of stress are more likely to exhibit mind-wandering, and as a result, their focus and cognitive performance decline [8][7]. These findings highlight the importance of creating systems that can help students not only improve attention but also detect and correct lapses in attention during exams [6].
[Heading 2] Physiological Measurement of Attention
[Normal] Physiological signals such as eye-gaze tracking and HRV have emerged as effective tools for measuring attention in real-time. Eye-gaze tracking measures where and for how long a person focuses their attention, providing key insights into attentional states. Metrics such as fixation duration, gaze stability, and saccadic movements allow systems to detect when a person’s attention is drifting from the task [9]. This technology can be particularly useful in identifying moments of off-task behaviour, providing an opportunity for intervention [6].
[Normal] In addition to eye-gaze tracking, HRV is another powerful measure of emotional regulation and cognitive flexibility. A higher HRV is associated with better self-regulation and adaptability, while lower HRV suggests stress and cognitive overload, both of which impair attention and focus [5][9]. By combining eye-gaze and HRV, systems can offer more comprehensive and accurate data on attentional states, allowing for timely interventions tailored to individual needs [9][10].
[Heading 2] Biofeedback and Self-Regulation Training
[Normal] Biofeedback systems offer valuable tools for improving self-regulation by allowing users to monitor and control their physiological states. Through the use of neurofeedback (EEG), HRV, and eye-gaze, individuals can gain insight into their emotional and cognitive states, learning to self-regulate their focus and attention. RelaxQuest, for example, uses EEG neurofeedback to guide users toward optimal brainwave patterns that promote relaxation and focus [5].
[Normal] Figure 2. Child plays RelaxQuest using EEG equipment. Sourced from [5]
[Normal] HRV biofeedback has proven especially effective for improving attention. By providing real-time feedback on HRV, users can learn to manage stress and regulate their emotional states, which in turn enhances their cognitive focus during tasks [5]. However, while EEG-based systems offer valuable insights into cognitive states, they can be complex and difficult to implement on a large scale. In contrast, HRV and eye-gaze provide more accessible alternatives for real-time feedback, ensuring effective and scalable self-regulation training [1].
[Heading 2] Immersive Technologies for Cognitive Training
[Normal] Immersive technologies such as virtual reality (VR) and mixed reality (MR) have emerged as powerful tools for cognitive and behavioural training. These technologies enhance engagement through presence, defined as the sensation of being immersed within a virtual environment. Higher levels of presence are associated with stronger cognitive and emotional responses, making immersive environments particularly effective for training purposes.
[Normal] VR has been widely used for cognitive training and rehabilitation, with studies showing its effectiveness in improving attention, memory, and executive function [4]. By providing controlled and interactive environments, VR allows users to practice cognitive skills in scenarios that closely resemble real-world conditions. This ability to simulate realistic environments is particularly valuable for training attention under distraction.
[Normal] The ARCADIA system, shown in Figure 2, demonstrates how mixed reality can support emotional regulation through gamified therapeutic activities [10]. MR combines real and virtual elements, allowing users to interact with both environments simultaneously. This integration enhances accessibility and flexibility by providing an immersive experience where physical and digital worlds blend seamlessly. MR can be particularly beneficial in therapeutic contexts, where users engage in virtual exercises while receiving real-world feedback, improving their ability to learn and adapt.
[Normal] The system’s adaptability ensures that it meets individual needs, making it accessible to users with diverse abilities. However, one of the major challenges in therapeutic MR applications is maintaining user engagement. Lack of adherence to treatment plans is common in therapy, and MR addresses this by incorporating interactive, game-like features to keep users motivated. Despite its benefits, sustaining user interest over time remains a key challenge, highlighting the need for designs that keep the experience enjoyable and dynamic.
[Normal] Figure 3. Gameplay Point of View of ARCADIA. Sourced from [10]
[Normal] Similarly, Stairway to Heaven, shown in Figure 3, demonstrates the effectiveness of VR combined with biofeedback and gamification [6]. The system incorporates respiratory sensors that track and monitor users' breathing patterns to control their progression through a virtual environment. As users interact with the virtual world, their ability to regulate their breathing directly impacts their movement and success within the environment, providing an interactive and engaging way to promote mindfulness. This approach encourages users to focus on their breath, fostering self-regulation by requiring conscious effort to maintain control over their respiratory patterns.
[Normal] Results from the study demonstrate that this method effectively promotes self-regulation, as users become more attuned to their breathing and better able to manage stress and anxiety. Additionally, the immersive nature of the virtual environment helps sustain users' attention on their breath, preventing mind-wandering and promoting consistent focus. This novel approach not only supports breathing regulation but also enhances overall mental engagement, making it a valuable tool for improving attention and emotional well-being in therapeutic settings.

[Heading 2] Just-In-Time Adaptive Interventions (JITAI) in System Design
[Normal] The Just-in-Time Adaptive Intervention (JITAI) framework offers a solid theoretical foundation for delivering personalized, context-aware support by ensuring that individuals receive the right type and amount of support at the right time [7]. Central to the JITAI approach is the idea of dynamically adapting interventions based on real-time data regarding an individual's continuously changing internal states (e.g., attentional lapses) and external conditions (e.g., distractions in the environment). This makes JITAI particularly relevant for applications that require constant attention regulation and cognitive performance under varying levels of stress and distraction, such as in VR-based training systems designed to improve concentration during academic exams [8].
[Normal] A key element of JITAI is the use of tailoring variables, which include real-time indicators of a user's physiological and behavioural states, such as heart rate variability (HRV) and eye-gaze patterns. These variables allow the system to personalize interventions, determining not only when a user needs support but also what type of intervention will be most effective. For instance, eye-gaze tracking can indicate whether a user’s focus has shifted from the task, while HRV can reveal emotional stress levels that may affect cognitive control. By continuously monitoring these variables, JITAI systems can make real-time adjustments, ensuring that the support provided is always in response to the user’s current attentional state rather than relying on a static or time-based schedule [9].
[Normal] In the context of VR-based training systems, JITAI is especially well-suited for training users to maintain focus and self-regulate their attention during simulated exam conditions. VR environments provide an immersive and controlled setting where users can experience distractions, both visual (e.g., invigilators walking past) and auditory (e.g., pen clicking, coughing), in a way that mimics real exam stress. The VR system, using JITAI principles, can detect when attention lapses occur and deliver timely interventions to help the user refocus [12]. For instance, when attention drift is detected through eye-gaze tracking, the system may trigger subtle visual cues (e.g., vignetting), auditory prompts, or haptic feedback to draw the user's attention back to the task without overwhelming them [10][9].
[Normal] A critical aspect of JITAI in system design is the balance between responsiveness and intrusiveness. While timely interventions are necessary to recover from attentional lapses, poorly timed or overly frequent interventions can disrupt engagement with the task, leading to intervention fatigue. In attention training scenarios, especially in high-stress conditions like exams, the goal is to support sustained focus without introducing additional distractions [12][7]. Thus, effective JITAI design requires careful calibration of detection thresholds, intervention frequency, and delivery modality. For example, systems must ensure that feedback is subtle and non-intrusive, while also being effective enough to guide users back to the task when needed [10].
[Normal] Furthermore, adaptive decision-making rules are vital in JITAI design. These rules determine not only when to intervene but also how to provide support based on the user’s evolving attentional and emotional state. For example, if a student’s focus is consistently low, the system might increase the frequency of interventions or adjust the difficulty of the task to provide more challenging distractions, progressively training the student’s ability to maintain attention under pressure [12][9]. This makes JITAI particularly effective in VR-based systems where the environment can adapt to the user’s needs in real time, offering personalized training that evolves with the user’s progress.
[Normal] By combining physiological monitoring with adaptive feedback, JITAI systems in VR not only help users improve their cognitive performance but also ensure that the interventions remain contextually relevant and non-disruptive. This approach allows for continuous improvement in attention regulation, providing users with the right amount of support at the optimal moment, helping them stay focused during exam-like conditions and reducing the likelihood of attentional lapses.

[Heading 2] Training Attention Under Distraction
[Normal] While previous research has demonstrated the effectiveness of biofeedback and immersive technologies in promoting self-regulation, there is a notable gap in systems specifically designed to train attention under realistic distraction conditions. This gap is particularly significant because real-world environments are often filled with competing stimuli, which challenge individuals' ability to maintain focus and attentional control [6][8].
[Normal] Virtual Reality (VR) offers a unique opportunity to address this gap by creating environments where distractions can be precisely controlled and manipulated. VR can simulate realistic scenarios in which individuals experience a variety of auditory (e.g., pen clicking, coughing) and visual (e.g., invigilators walking by) distractions. These immersive environments allow users to train their ability to maintain attention despite such distractions, providing an interactive platform for improving focus under stressful conditions [12][14]. By incorporating auditory and visual distractions that mimic real-world environments (such as work or study settings), VR enables individuals to practice focusing in contexts that are highly relevant to their daily lives [5][10].
[Normal] Furthermore, VR systems can adapt in real-time to the user’s performance, increasing the difficulty of tasks as users improve their attentional control. By adjusting the level of distractions in response to the user’s attentional state, these systems personalize the training experience, ensuring that it becomes progressively more challenging as the user’s skills develop. This approach is grounded in adaptive learning principles, where the training tasks evolve based on the individual's current capabilities and progress [9][7]. Gradually increasing the complexity of distractions allows users to refine their attentional control in a dynamic, personalized environment that ensures continued engagement and effectiveness [12][14].
[Normal] In summary, by providing a controlled yet dynamic training environment that adapts to a user’s progress, VR offers an innovative solution for improving attentional control under distraction-rich conditions. This personalized approach ensures that the training remains relevant, challenging, and effective for enhancing cognitive performance [5][9].
[Heading 2] Research Gap
[Normal] The literature demonstrates that attention is highly vulnerable to stress and cognitive load, particularly in demanding environments such as examinations. At the same time, physiological signals have been shown to enable real-time detection of attentional states, while biofeedback systems can support improvements in self-regulation. Immersive technologies further enhance user engagement and training effectiveness, and Just-In-Time Adaptive Intervention (JITAI) frameworks provide a mechanism for delivering adaptive and personalised support.
[Normal] Despite these advancements, the approaches remain largely fragmented. Existing systems tend to focus on relaxation, emotional regulation, or general cognitive training, rather than directly addressing attention regulation under realistic and distraction-rich conditions.
[Normal] In particular, there is limited research that integrates multimodal physiological monitoring, such as eye tracking and heart rate variability, with real-time adaptive interventions based on attentional state. There is also a lack of systems that combine immersive environments with controlled distractions and apply these within high-pressure, performance-based contexts. This highlights a clear gap in the development of unified systems that effectively combine detection, adaptation, and immersive training to improve concentration in real-world scenarios.
